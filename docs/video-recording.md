# Chattogram demo recording guide

**Audience question:** After the flood, where do you check first?

**What the app helps someone do:** Compare the same area through cloud cover, choose a place worth checking and share the evidence behind that choice. Tell the story from the point of view of a local reviewer deciding where to look next.

Open `/chattogram/tour?source=frozen` and select **Presentation view**. Use **Next** or the arrow keys to hold each shot until the narration is done. The saved case uses the committed imagery. The map still needs its configured Mapbox basemap.

| Edit time | Show | Suggested narration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:25 | Your street and crop-field flood footage. Caption each clip with its place, date and credit where known. | “Flooding can block roads and damage crops. Once the water rises, someone still has to work out where to check first. From above, the same monsoon clouds can hide the places they need to see.” |
| 0:25–0:55 | Tour 1. Drag from the satellite camera toward radar. | “Here’s Chattogram on one cloudy morning. The camera sees cloud. NASA and ISRO’s NISAR satellite sends out radar pulses and records the echoes. That lets us see the ground through those clouds.” |
| 0:55–1:20 | Tour 2. Hold on the Karnaphuli study outline and the port. | “We tested that idea along the Karnaphuli. This outline covers the river, the port and nearby land. It gives us one defined area to compare.” |
| 1:20–2:00 | Tour 3. Sweep the June–October slider once. Leave both dates visible. | “Here are two radar views of the same place, one in June and one in October. As we move the divider, some patches brighten and others darken. The dates are a seasonal example of the workflow.” |
| 2:00–2:50 | Tours 4–5. Show the change colours, then zoom into the selected patch. | “The map points us to places where the radar return changed. Let’s take this one. Its radar echo grew stronger. That could be plants growing, or the soil could have become wetter. Now we have a specific place and two dates to check against a field report or a dated photo.” |
| 2:50–3:35 | Tours 6–7. Open the linked evidence and point to the selected patch and its sources. | “The app keeps the measured signal, possible explanations and source dates together. A local reviewer can save the brief and pass the evidence along. Confirming a flood or crop loss requires an independent check.” |
| 3:35–4:00 | End on the highlighted patch, then the team, source credits and project link. | “CryoCore Horizon turns radar observations into places people can follow up on. Start with the map, choose a place to check and take the evidence with you.” |

The footage establishes the stakes. Introduce the app shots with an on-screen caption such as **“Karnaphuli seasonal comparison · 18 June and 16 October 2026 UTC.”** This keeps the footage distinct from the satellite example. If you show a number, open **Show the radar measurements** on the selected patch. Use the dates and values on screen.

For a quick rehearsal, have someone outside the team watch the intro through the first swipe. Ask them to tell you what the app helps them decide. If they say “which places to check and why,” move on to recording.
